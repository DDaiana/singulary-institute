# Institutional Question

Why do AI policy and innovation outputs fail at the point of use, and what institutional mechanism could convert them into decision-ready tools under conditions of stress?

This project examines the gap between policy production and practical application. Governments, research institutions, and innovation bodies produce an increasing volume of AI-related frameworks, reports, and strategic documents. These outputs are designed to guide decision-making, yet they are rarely used as such by those responsible for implementation.

The problem becomes visible under conditions of stress: rapid technological change, regulatory uncertainty, and time-constrained decision environments. In these contexts, decision-makers must act quickly but cannot process high-volume, high-complexity information. When policy outputs are not structured for rapid interpretation, they are effectively excluded from the decision process.

The central tradeoff is that institutions optimize for completeness and legitimacy, while users require compression and relevance.

This project treats that gap as an institutional design problem.
